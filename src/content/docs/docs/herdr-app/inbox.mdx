The inbox is a drawer at the right that `INBOX` (or `forgerdr.inbox`) opens and its ✕ closes. It holds what waits on your mark in every session, a card each, the oldest first, with filters for permissions, questions, plans and the forgerdr's gates.

<img src={withBase("screens/05-inbox.png")} width="1440" height="900" alt="The inbox drawer: a plan to approve, a commit gate, a permission, and the comments waiting in their views." />

## What it holds

| Card | What it shows | Your answer |
|---|---|---|
| Permission | every field of what the tool runs, whole (a file's new content, an edit's old and new text, a command's flags), and why | `Allow once`, `Deny with reason` |
| Question | its options (several on a multi-select one, a draft beside the one picked when it carries one) and an answer of your own | `Answer` |
| Gate | a question headed `Gate commit`, `Gate push`, `Gate PR`, `Gate comment`, `Gate state` or `Gate item`, with its draft | `Stamp` |
| Plan | the plan as plain text, with the mode its session takes after it | `Approve plan`, `Revise` with what to change |
| An agent waiting | an agent waiting in its pane with nothing the inbox can answer | the card takes you to its pane |

Each card shows the gnome and the name of the forge it comes from, then the branch and the session, and how long it has waited. `Discuss` takes you to its pane and leaves the decision open.

## Answering a card

It is all clicks. The card open stays open when a newer one comes, and a click approves only once it has been open half a second, counted again each time the drawer opens, so a double click, a card that moved or a drawer just opened never approves one you did not see.

## The stamp

An answer stamps the card with the gnomemark, gold for a yes and red for a no, with the stamp's thump: the card trembles under the blow, then slides off to the right, and the cards below rise into its place. With the motion off, only the sound.

Claude Code's own dialog stays open in the pane meanwhile, and whichever answers first wins: a card answered in its pane goes. The open card's ✕ takes a request out with no answer, for one answered elsewhere that stayed.

## Questions typed in the chat

A session whose final message asks a question typed in the chat, which no inbox sees, is sent back once to ask it as a question with options. An agent the forgerdr dispatched asks the session that dispatched it instead.

:::caution[A convenience, not a lock]
Anything running as you can write an answer to the inbox, as it can type in a pane. The drawer saves you from hunting prompts in panes you are not looking at; it is not a security boundary.
:::

Outside the herdr app nothing changes: Claude Code's dialogs work as always.
